Electronic Stability Control

A car on a slippery road is steered through a slalom. The ESC unit compares the yaw rate the driver asked for with the yaw rate a gyro reports, and brakes one wheel to close the gap. The gyro signal is noisy, so a digital filter cleans it first. Change the filter, the noise and the road, then watch what the controller does with the result.

Try this

1 · No filter, noisy gyro

Feed the raw signal back and raise the noise. The yaw-moment chart turns into a buzz, because noise goes straight through the proportional gain. Real brake valves would chatter.

2 · Filter cutoff at 1 Hz

The noise is gone, but the filtered trace now lags and shrinks compared with the true yaw rate. The controller reacts late, and the yaw-rate error grows, even past the no-ESC case. Filtering costs phase, so cutoff is a trade-off.

3 · Icy road, high speed

Drop μ and raise speed. The car loses grip without ESC. The reference yaw rate is limited to what the road can support, so ESC asks for less than the driver steers.

Model: single-track vehicle (1500 kg, Iz 2500 kg·m², wheelbase 2.7 m) at constant speed with saturating tyre forces, a PI yaw-rate controller running at 200 Hz, a 2nd-order Butterworth low-pass filter and an ideal brake modulator limited to ±6 kN·m. It is a teaching model, not a calibrated vehicle. Road-view lateral distance and sideslip angle are exaggerated so they can be seen; the scale is shown above the lanes.
